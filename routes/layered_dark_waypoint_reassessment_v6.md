# Layered dark waypoints — reassessment after the Hospital Real model

Controller correction, 3 October 2026. The supplied Hospital Real finale exposes
a second bias in the earlier Paris screening: it treated “physical payoff” too
often as a visible plaque, surviving crime room, grave, machine or officially
interpreted object. That standard is useful for ordinary history routes but too
narrow for mystery.

A barred historic building, a dark window, a documented parcel, a replaced
façade, a sealed tunnel, or even the tension between current calm and an earlier
use can be a strong physical encounter. The site earns the stop when **documented
history and traceable legend interact with what the walker can see now**.

The Hospital Real narration is accepted as a second target model, alongside the
San Luis 39 structure in `mystery_narration_protocol_v6.md`.

## Why the Hospital Real narration works

It does not depend on seeing a ghost, entering the building or finding a plaque
that says “haunted.” It builds power through convergence.

### 1. The building carries documented functions

Hospital, hospice, asylum, confinement, treatment, poverty and death are not
invented atmosphere. They establish what the walls were built to contain.

### 2. A named person passes through the institution twice

Juan Ciudad first appears as a confined patient subjected to period treatment.
He returns as an active rescuer during the fire. That reversal gives the site an
arc before folklore begins.

### 3. The legend transforms rather than merely decorates the history

The reported ghost is not a random monk added to a suitably old building. The
tradition turns the former patient and later caregiver into someone still making
the rounds. The supernatural layer interprets the documented biography.

### 4. The present site supplies an imaginative frame

Bars, façade, courtyards and dark windows are enough because the listener knows
what happened behind them and what later accounts locate there. “Look at a dark
window” is a real onsite action even though no apparition can be promised.

### 5. It closes the entire route

The finale recalls the exorcism death, forgotten necropolis, condemned daughter,
court, execution ground and garden. Hospital Real is not simply one more haunted
building; it resolves the route’s argument about how documented death becomes
urban memory and story.

### 6. It grants temporary imaginative permission

The ending openly proposes suspension of disbelief. It does not retract the
evidence boundaries. The fox/guide simply declines to close the final ambiguity.

## Revised physical-payoff rule

For a dark or mystery waypoint, **physical payoff does not require an object that
proves the story**. It may consist of:

- a surviving building connected to the event;
- exact ground beneath a replacement building;
- a gate, bars, courtyard, window line, street alignment or sealed entrance;
- a monument repurposed by public ritual;
- a documented absence or demolition;
- a visible institution that preserves the type of practice involved;
- the contrast between present use and earlier violence, confinement, belief or
  catastrophe;
- a view that lets the walker understand above/below, inside/outside, public/private
  or care/confinement relationships.

The question is not “can I see the ghost/crime room?” It is:

> **Does standing here change how the documented event and later story can be
> understood?**

If yes, the site may pass even when access ends at a gate.

## Layered-site viability test

A mystery waypoint is viable when at least four of the following six elements
are strong, and either element 1 or 2 is present:

1. **Documented human event or institutional history.**
2. **Traceable testimony, legend, ritual or cultural afterlife.**
3. **Exact or honestly bounded ground.**
4. **Present visual frame**, including meaningful absence.
5. **Transformation or reversal** between historical roles or later meanings.
6. **Route function**—opening, complication, thematic turn or payoff.

An actual ghost marker is not a seventh requirement.

## Paris waypoints reopened by this rule

“Reopen” means worthy of cheap evidence/physical audit. It does not mean every
candidate is now accepted or that earlier negative evidence disappears.

### 1. Nerval memorial / Tour Saint-Jacques / lost Vieille-Lanterne

**Old status:** optional prelude, weakened because the alley vanished and the
memorial is not a ghost report.

**New status: REOPEN AS STRONG LAYERED WAYPOINT.**

- Documented death near a subsequently erased alley.
- Visible memorial and tower.
- Nerval’s supernatural literary Paris and Doré’s allegorical treatment.
- City demolition creates absence that the listener can compare with the broad
  present space.
- The ambiguity is not whether a ghost was legally proven; it is how death,
  literature, image and demolition make one place haunt another.

It may now function as a substantial central mystery stop rather than merely a
distance-disfavored prelude. Geography still prevents silently attaching it to
the Père-Lachaise core; use transit or build a separate central sequence.

### 2. Former Capucines phantasmagoria ground

**Old status:** replaced by Robertson’s tomb because the performance setting
vanished.

**New status: REOPEN FOR EXACT-PARCEL AUDIT, not automatic duplication.**

The historical reversal is excellent: a religious building emptied through
Revolutionary transformation became a paid machinery-of-ghosts setting. If the
exact footprint and a useful present street/building frame can be established,
the absence itself can introduce manufactured haunting before Robertson’s tomb.
The two stops must perform different jobs: site/institutional transformation
versus surviving self-memorial.

### 3. Cour des Miracles

**Old status:** rejected because the court vanished and hostile literary/social
stereotypes supplied most of the surviving story.

**New status: REOPEN AS A MYTH-OF-CRIME WAYPOINT.**

The hostile stereotype is not merely a source defect; it may be the subject.
Exact ground, disappearance, early description and Hugo’s later transformation
could show how a city invented an organized criminal underworld from fear of
poverty, disability and deception. It belongs only if the script exposes the
construction of the legend rather than presenting the “beggar kingdom” as fact.

### 4. Saint-Martin and Croix-Rouge ghost stations

**Old status:** rejected because platforms are not lawfully visible from the
surface.

**New status: REOPEN CONDITIONALLY.**

A sealed entrance, ventilation trace, street alignment or view from lawful
transit may be enough if the route can establish the stations’ actual histories
and trace when “ghost station” narratives appeared. The stop fails if it offers
only “something invisible is below us” with no exact surface relationship.

### 5. Trocadéro clandestine cinema

**Old status:** rejected because the underground room is inaccessible and
reported details may contain embellishment or spoofing.

**New status: REOPEN AS A DISPUTED-MODERN-MYSTERY STOP.**

Inaccessibility and contested reporting are the point. Above is one of Paris’s
most visible tourist stages; below was a reported unauthorized cinema whose
discovery story, removal and possible mythmaking remain disputed. The script can
separate police/reporting claims, later skepticism and what the esplanade cannot
show. It must not give access instructions or pretend the chamber survives.

### 6. Petiot, 21 rue Le Sueur

**Old status:** case essential, street waypoint weak because the original house
was demolished.

**New status: KEEP AS A MEANINGFUL-ABSENCE CANDIDATE.**

The replacement building can support a strong question: what does the city mark,
erase or allow to become ordinary after a notorious crime? Archival images and
the current façade can be compared without pretending the murder house survives.
This remains optional until safe viewpoint and current-building history are
verified.

### 7. Violette Nozière, 9 rue de Madagascar

**Status strengthened.**

The current residential exterior need not be the preserved apartment. Street
scale, railway-worker context, media afterlife and the conflict among poisoner,
daughter, alleged abuse victim, surrealist emblem and rehabilitated offender
make the address physically and narratively useful.

### 8. Diana / Flamme de la Liberté

**Status strengthened.**

The flame’s original purpose, visitor repurposing and formal place naming are a
textbook transformation: the monument does not prove a theory about the crash;
it proves that public ritual changed what the site means.

### 9. Square Henri-Galli / relocated Bastille stones

**Old status:** removed because it made a backward tail and was weaker than
Nation as Revolutionary’s ending.

**New status: viable object, still wrong in that route position.**

The stones are not discarded evidence. They may work in a compact material-
memory sequence about demolition, relocation and the desire for authentic
fragments. Narrative viability does not override bad geometry or steal Nation’s
payoff.

### 10. Picpus

**Old status:** rejected from the paranormal route because no rooted apparition
tradition was found.

**New status: strong religious/death-memory waypoint, not falsely paranormal.**

The layered model does not require every dark site to have a ghost. Burial pits,
prayer, martyr commemoration, family cemetery and restricted access can create a
powerful religious horror/memory stop. It belongs in a death, Terror or sacred-
memory module unless traceable supernatural tradition is later found.

## Candidates not automatically revived

The revised rule is generous, not indiscriminate.

- **Vampire of Montparnasse:** the case is real, but the exact capture/incident
  ground remains insufficiently fixed. “Somewhere in this cemetery” is still too
  loose unless the narration is explicitly about the press label and geographic
  uncertainty.
- **Generic execution ghosts:** documented death does not automatically create a
  traceable legend. A route may discuss how later tours invented one, but must
  locate that invention.
- **Generic filmmaker/celebrity homes:** association alone still lacks event,
  transformation or mystery function.
- **Every unusual old building:** age, bars and darkness do not independently
  establish a story.
- **Anonymous online haunting claims:** they remain Tier D until their circulation
  can be dated or contextualized.

## Route-design consequence

The portfolio should stop asking every waypoint to be a self-contained exhibit.
A strong mystery route can alternate:

1. a hard documented event;
2. an exact but altered place;
3. a witness or institutional account;
4. a legend growing around that account;
5. a quiet physical frame;
6. a finale where the route’s earlier layers converge.

This makes previously “weak” addresses valuable when they perform transition,
absence, inversion or synthesis. It does **not** turn ordinary connector distance
into thematic mileage; the site must still change the argument.

## Finale protocol

The Hospital Real example also changes how endings are judged. A dark-route
finale may pass when it:

- has its own documented event and legend;
- recalls earlier waypoints without summarizing them mechanically;
- changes the apparent role of the ghost, victim, institution or building;
- offers one final present-tense act of looking;
- gives permission for temporary imaginative play;
- and ends before explaining the last image away.

The guide voice may be playful. “The fox will not tell you what it was” is valid
because the script has already done the evidentiary work. Playfulness at the end
is earned by precision earlier.

## Controller ruling

This reassessment supersedes any earlier conclusion that “no surviving object”
or “no visible paranormal marker” automatically means no waypoint. The new rule
is:

> **A mystery site earns its place when documented history, traceable story and
> present physical frame transform one another—even if what survives is a gate,
> a replacement façade, a sealed entrance, or an absence.**
