# Twentieth-century and contemporary true crime — corrective discovery

Controller correction, 3 October 2026. The user is right: the portfolio’s dark
material had become too nineteenth-century. Robertson, Kardec and Victor Noir
remain an excellent signature experience, but they do not satisfy an interest
in twentieth-century and contemporary crime, forensic investigation, policing,
trials and the manufacture of the *fait divers*.

The answer should not be only a checklist of apartments where people were
killed. The strongest publicly legible Paris material supports a compact,
institution-led experience about **how a crime becomes evidence, a case and a
story**. That framing must not sanitize the cases or pretend that institutional
findings eliminate every uncertainty. Mystery—the gap between established fact,
reasonable inference, unresolved question and later myth—is part of the route.

# From Crime Story to Case File — Paris, Evidence and the Myth of “36”

**Status: PROTECTED MODERN TRUE-CRIME MODULE / operationally conditional.**

**Core:** BiLiPo → Musée de la Préfecture de Police → former 36 quai des
Orfèvres. **1.545 km routed**, approximately 3–4 hours with two deliberate

It earns a place because the physical encounters are unusually strong:

- an institution that preserves crime fiction, criminology, criminalistics,
  press dossiers and the history of crime narratives;
- a police museum with collections extending into celebrated twentieth-century
  cases and the history of scientific identification;
- the former institutional address whose investigators handled cases including
  Violette Nozière, Petiot, Mesrine and Guy Georges;
- an optional **transit coda**, not walking mileage, at the new 36 rue du Bastion
  and Tribunal de Paris, showing where investigation and first-instance justice
  moved in 2017–18.

## Narrative progression

### 1. BiLiPo — a crime becomes a story

**Address:** 48 rue du Cardinal-Lemoine.

The Bibliothèque des littératures policières is not a themed bookshop. The City
describes it as a specialized institution conserving crime and espionage
literature alongside reference works on criminology, criminalistics, criminal
cases, police and justice. Its documentary practice includes press material and
the long exchange between actual cases and fictional forms.

Narrative question: before the police file reaches an archive or a case becomes
a streaming series, who selects the documents, headlines, photographs and plot
that make the public think it knows what happened?

This opening prevents “true crime” from meaning only standing outside a murder
address. It makes mediation itself visible. Do not promise a particular dossier,
rare book or exhibition case without advance confirmation. The normal encounter
is consultation/collection orientation, not free handling of archival evidence.

Current official 15 September–31 December 2026 hours are Tuesday–Friday
14:00–18:00 and Saturday 10:00–17:00. The City lists public accessibility and
onsite consultation. Confirm reader-card/identity and requested-document rules
before writing the finite raid.

Source: [Ville de Paris, BiLiPo](https://www.paris.fr/lieux/bibliotheque-des-litteratures-policieres-bilipo-2879).

### 2. Musée de la Préfecture de Police — a person becomes evidence

**Address:** 4 rue de la Montagne-Sainte-Geneviève, inside a functioning police
building.

The museum provides the material chapter that scattered crime scenes cannot:
objects, images and institutional interpretation about policing and celebrated
cases. Current visitor information describes more than 2,000 pieces and names
Landru and Petiot among the criminal histories represented. Separate official
heritage programming confirms that the institution presents scientific-police
and criminal-investigation material; Archives de Paris documents the wider
history of face/profile identification associated with Bertillon.

Narrative question: what changes when suspicion is converted into a standardized
photograph, measurement, trace, report or object—and what biases can be hidden
inside something presented as objective?

This must not become institutional triumphalism. Bertillonage’s power to make
people administratively comparable is part of the story; so are uncertainty,
error and the difference between an exhibit and proof of guilt. A finite 2026
object list still requires a current-room audit. Do not promise Landru’s oven,
Petiot artifacts or any internet-famous object until the museum itself confirms
that it is displayed.

The museum is free but uses a reservation system and security inside an active
commissariat. Current regional visitor information gives weekday hours plus the
first and third Saturday; the official booking page must control the actual
visit.

Sources: [official museum booking page](https://www.smartagenda.fr/pro/prefecture-de-police-musee/rendez-vous/),
[Paris Region visitor record](https://www.visitparisregion.com/fr/musee-de-la-prefecture-de-police),
[Archives de Paris on identification photography](https://archives.paris.fr/offre-culturelle/expositions-virtuelles/petite-histoire-sommaire-de-la-photographie-a-travers-les-fonds-des-archives-de-paris/variete-des-supports-photographiques-aux-archives-de-paris-1).

### 3. Former 36 quai des Orfèvres — a case becomes an institution and a myth

The old Police judiciaire address supplies the exterior payoff. The City’s own
account connects more than a century at “36” to Violette Nozière, Stavisky,
Petiot, the Empain kidnapping, Mesrine and the pursuit of Guy Georges, while
also emphasizing how cinema and literature mythologized the address.

Narrative question: when the headquarters itself becomes a character, what is
remembered—the investigators, the accused, the victims, an institutional myth,
or the screen version?

Observe only from ordinary public space. The former headquarters is not a crime
theme park, an invitation to enter a secure court/police complex, or proof that
every famous investigation occurred in the visible room behind a particular
window. Do not retell graphic victim details merely because a case name is
famous.

Source: [Ville de Paris, the move from quai des Orfèvres to Bastion](https://www.paris.fr/pages/nouvelles-enquetes-aux-batignolles-pour-le-36-5133).

## Optional contemporary coda — use transit

The Police judiciaire moved to **36 rue du Bastion** in 2017, beside the new
Tribunal de Paris, which opened in 2018. Official court material describes more
than 1,700 police, scientific and administrative staff consolidated there and a
secure underground connection used for transfers to the court.

This is a strong twenty-first-century conclusion about scale, security and the
physical integration of police and justice. It is **not** a reason to count the
roughly cross-city movement as thematic walking. Take transit, view only from
public space and never approach secure entrances or photograph operational
security arrangements.

## Mystery protocol — do not turn uncertainty into either conspiracy or polish

The controlling delivery standard is now `mystery_narration_protocol_v6.md`.
The four evidence levels below are its compact case-file notation, not a demand
to remove testimony, folklore or necessary disturbing detail.

Victim-conscious narration does **not** mean telling only a reassuring official
version. It means refusing to exploit suffering while still allowing the route
to examine contradiction, incomplete evidence, disputed interpretation and the
public appetite for an unresolved story.

Every case chapter should use four explicit evidence levels:

1. **Established:** supported by a judgment, inquiry finding, primary archive or
   uncontested physical evidence.
2. **Reported:** attributed to a named contemporary source, witness or press
   account, without being silently promoted to fact.
3. **Disputed or unresolved:** competing claims exist, evidence did not settle
   the issue, or legal proceedings answered a narrower question than the public
   one.
4. **Myth, adaptation or conspiracy claim:** culturally important because people
   repeat it, not evidence that it happened.

The guide should say “we do not know” when that is true. It should also say “the
record does establish this” when mystery culture erases an inconvenient fact.
The aim is neither pink-tinted commemoration nor transgressive certainty. The
route’s pleasure comes partly from testing what kind of knowledge each object
can actually support.

### What is physically and historically legible

- Diana died following the car crash in the Alma tunnel during the night of
  30–31 August 1997; Dodi Fayed and driver Henri Paul also died, while Trevor
  Rees-Jones survived.
- The **Flamme de la Liberté was inaugurated in 1989 for an entirely different
  purpose**, connected to the Statue of Liberty restoration relationship. After
  the crash, visitors transformed it into an unofficial memorial through
  flowers, photographs and messages.
- In 2019 the City formally named and extended **place Diana** to the parapet
  above the tunnel. The official naming dossier explicitly records both the
  crash and the public repurposing of the pre-existing flame.
- French proceedings examined involuntary homicide, injuries, failure to assist
  and privacy questions. Later litigation concerning photographs did not answer
  every broader moral or causal question people attach to the event.

### The actual mystery chapter

Begin with the object’s double identity. The flame was not built for Diana, yet
collective use made it her memorial before the City’s naming decision formalized
that association. Then separate questions that are too often collapsed:

- What happened physically in the tunnel?
- What did French criminal and privacy proceedings decide?
- What conduct by the driver, photographers, passengers or institutions was
  investigated, alleged, established or left outside a particular judgment?
- Why did an accidental-death site become fertile ground for conspiracy claims?
- When thousands of visitors repurpose a monument, does repeated belief change
  the monument’s meaning even when it does not change the event’s facts?

Do not recite a single totalizing answer. Build a small evidence ledger onsite:
**fact / legal finding / disputed inference / later myth**. Conspiracy claims may
be named when they illuminate how uncertainty is manufactured, but they must be
paired with the evidence that supports or contradicts them. “Unresolved” is not
a synonym for “any theory is equally likely.”

This waypoint joins modern true crime, media ethics and memorial culture. It is
also the clearest contemporary counterpart to Victor Noir: in each case a real
death acquired a public ritual at a physical object, but the media systems,
scale and uncertainties are radically different.

## Why not an indiscriminate serial-killer-address walk

Twentieth-century Paris supplies many notorious names: Landru, Violette Nozière,
Petiot, Mesrine, Thierry Paulin, Guy Georges and others. Fame alone does not make
their homes, arrest locations or victim sites responsible visitor waypoints.
Common failure modes include:

- no marker or surviving evidence at the address;
- a private home whose current residents have no relationship to the case;
- an address that centers the perpetrator while erasing victims;
- graphic narration used as atmosphere;
- uncertain pins repeated from unsourced “dark tourism” lists;
- unresolved or contested evidence flattened into a confident story;
- active memorial sites treated as entertainment.

The institutional route can still discuss Petiot, Nozière, Mesrine or Guy Georges
when an actual museum object, police history or archival dossier supports the
chapter. It should ask how evidence and public narratives were made, not invite
the walker to collect killers’ façades.

This is **not a blanket ban on street addresses**. `true_crime_across_eras_v6.md`
now promotes Violette Nozière’s exact rue de Madagascar ground for investigation
and route-tests a northbound Landru/Bonnot branch. A private or replaced building
can carry a chapter when exact ground, documented absence and unresolved evidence
are themselves the point; it fails only when the address contributes nothing
beyond notoriety.
