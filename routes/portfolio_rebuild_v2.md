# Paris Walking Portfolio Rebuild v2 — Theme First

Working architecture, 3 October 2026. This rebuild starts from the waypoint pool rather than the twenty inherited route labels. It is not a source audit, final geometry, operating calendar, or external QA result. Museum interiors are excluded here; free-museum-day planning remains a separate layer.

## Design rule

The portfolio is built around **walking days**, not around preserving old files. A route earns a day when it has:

1. one or more physically persuasive anchors;
2. a thesis that changes how the walker reads the places;
3. enough distinct chapters for roughly 7–11 km without padding;
4. a meaningful opening, development, and payoff;
5. an identity that does not collapse into another route.

Geography is connective tissue. It becomes the primary subject only for landscapes—canals, parks, railway margins, promenades—where the physical transformation itself is the story.

Target: **20 real walking days from 28 theoretical half-day-ish slots**, while accepting a practical executed range of roughly 17–21 after friend time, bed/rest days, weather, and free-museum day. Several routes can degrade to compact modules; none should be stretched merely to fill a day.

### C. Famous landmarks that require a real thematic owner

- **Notre-Dame:** medieval devils and craftsmanship, not generic cathedral tourism.
- **Louvre:** revolutionary palace edge and modern screen-haunting; no greatest-hits interior.
- **Eiffel Tower:** modern image-making and western river/industrial landscape, not a standalone stop.
- **Arc de Triomphe / Champs-Élysées:** public victory, national spectacle, and Mata Hari’s hotel block; it must earn a chapter inside a state/surveillance walk.
- **Sacré-Cœur / Montmartre:** contested hilltop image and performance district; avoid a generic “bohemian Montmartre” walk.
- **Palais Garnier:** theatre machinery, public display, and Phantom cultural afterlife; candidate for the performance route.
- **Invalides / École Militaire:** military display and Dreyfus/public certainty; candidate for Spy/State, not a monuments checklist.
- **Panthéon:** republican selection and monumental authority; exterior belongs to the Republic route.
- **Place des Vosges / Marais:** only as connective urban form unless a stronger exact story emerges.
- **Grande Arche:** retains a valid cosmopolitan/institutional thesis but does not justify a remote route by itself; reserve, not mandatory coverage.

## Proposed portfolio — 20 walking days

### 1. Bargains with the Invisible — Medieval Devils and Miracles

**Thesis:** Old Paris gave invisible forces physical addresses through charity, craft, royal sacred authority, and contested cures; later storytellers rewrote those addresses.

**Anchor:** Notre-Dame’s devil doors.

**Spine:** Flamel house → Tour Saint-Jacques passing handoff → Notre-Dame western portals → Sainte-Chapelle exterior/palace perimeter → Saint-Médard.

**Approximate walk:** 7–8 km.

**Why it earns a route:** Three excellent surviving exterior anchors plus the Cité’s sacred/political setting create a chronological and physical arc. The extra distance comes from meaningful medieval-city transitions, not occult filler.

**Absorbs/kills:** absorbs old Route 02; Tour Saint-Jacques becomes connective context. Kills Cour des Miracles and any paid Sainte-Chapelle dependence.

### 2. The Dead Become Public — Spirits, Spectacle, and Cemetery Ritual

**Thesis:** Nineteenth-century Paris turned the dead into projected entertainment, organized doctrine, political memory, and objects of continuing request.

**Anchor:** Père-Lachaise trio—Robertson, Kardec, Victor Noir.

**Spine:** Nerval/Tour Saint-Jacques prelude → eastward city walk through République/Belleville connective streets → Père-Lachaise: Robertson → Kardec → Victor Noir.

**Approximate walk:** 8–10 km including cemetery paths.

**Why it earns a route:** The three cemetery monuments are unusually strong physical narrators; Nerval supplies the literary opening and the long eastward movement becomes a deliberate journey from erased central Paris to the monumental city of the dead.

**Absorbs/kills:** absorbs old Route 03 and the strongest literary handoff from Route 02. Does not borrow Catacombs or generic graves.

### 3. The City Under the City — Quarries, Horror, and Invented Underworlds

**Thesis:** Paris’s real underground geology and managed entrances repeatedly become raw material for monsters, crime panics, and cinematic labyrinths.

**Anchor:** Catacombs/Denfert threshold and Montsouris quarry relief.

**Spine:** Montparnasse Cemetery “vampire” context → Denfert Catacombs exterior/Guillaumot → René-Coty exit context → Petite Ceinture/rail-edge connector → Parc Montsouris quarry relief → Butte-aux-Cailles high ground.

**Approximate walk:** 7–9 km.

**Why it earns a route:** It combines a major famous attraction’s urban meaning with lawful surface evidence, geology, nineteenth-century press horror, and a visible landscape shaped by unstable ground. The Catacombs interior remains a separate paid activity, not required here.

**Absorbs/kills:** absorbs the exterior/surface half of old Route 14 and the Montparnasse reserve from Route 03. Kills ghost-station padding and clandestine-access fantasy.

### 4. Monsters in Plain Sight — Modern Paris After Dark

**Thesis:** Modern media makes monsters believable behind prestigious façades, while ordinary infrastructure occasionally produces a true story stranger than fiction.

**Anchor:** Louvre/Belphégor and Éléonore beneath rue du Pont-Neuf.

**Spine:** Palais Garnier/Phantom exterior context → Louvre courts/Belphégor → rue du Pont-Neuf crocodile site → Châtelet’s hidden-infrastructure streets → 145 rue La Fayette false residential façade.

**Approximate walk:** 7–8 km.

**Why it earns a route:** The previous two-stop module becomes viable when joined to two strong “building conceals another city” anchors. It remains about twentieth-century media and invisible urban systems, not medieval folklore.

**Absorbs/kills:** rebuilds old Route 04; absorbs one Illusions anchor. Kills closed ghost stations as stops, Les Halles nostalgia, Trocadéro clandestine cinema, and Catacombs duplication.

### 5. When the Street Took Power — Revolutionary Paris

**Thesis:** Commercial and ceremonial spaces became stages where crowds challenged sovereignty, experienced victory, and faced the return of authority.

**Anchor:** Palais-Royal to Bastille sequence.

**Spine:** Palais-Royal café de Foy → Concorde → Tuileries/Carrousel → Hôtel de Ville → Bastille → Henri-Galli masonry epilogue.

**Approximate walk:** 8–9 km.

**Why it earns a route:** Five strong public-space chapters already form one of the portfolio’s best arcs; the famous landmarks are evidence in an argument rather than trophies.

**Absorbs/kills:** preserves old Route 06. Kills generic revolutionary-person plaques and the distant Père-Lachaise Commune tail.

### 6. Who Gets to Be France? — Republic, Representation, and Recognition

**Thesis:** The Republic makes promises in sculpture, selects exemplary citizens, and conducts representative government inside inherited monumental forms.

**Anchor:** République and Panthéon.

**Spine:** Nation/Dalou → République monument → Panthéon exterior → Luxembourg/Sénat exterior → Palais Bourbon/Pont de la Concorde.

**Approximate walk:** 10–11 km, with the Nation→République leg earning its place through the contrast between social movement and electoral symbolism.

**Why it earns a route:** Restoring Nation gives the route enough physical breadth and a sharper opening comparison; it remains distinct from revolutionary events.

**Absorbs/kills:** rebuilds old Route 05. Hôtel de Ville remains with Route 5 above; no Panthéon interior assumed.

### 7. Occupied City — Constraint, Collaboration, Resistance, Absence

**Thesis:** Occupation transformed ordinary documents, buildings, journeys, and addresses; resistance and remembrance did not erase unequal survival.

**Anchor:** Lutetia and 48 rue du Four, with the Shoah memorial exterior complex as ending.

**Spine:** Denfert wartime command-site exterior → occupation/daily-life street context → Lutetia → 48 rue du Four → Vel d’Hiv memory marker or another exact roundup trace if geometry supports it → Shoah memorial exterior/Mur des Justes.

**Approximate walk:** 8–10 km.

**Why it earns a route:** It preserves the excellent moral and narrative structure without relying on museum interiors. One new exact street-level persecution anchor is needed before final acceptance.

**Absorbs/kills:** rebuilds old Route 07 exterior-first. Kills the triumphal Hôtel de Ville liberation ending and museum-object dependence.

### 8. Who Reads Your Papers? — Spy Paris

**Thesis:** Espionage works through privileged access, copied documents, trusted intermediaries, institutional certainty, and public legends that obscure operational reality.

**Anchor:** Hôtel Beauharnais/Dreyfus evidence chain.

**Spine:** Franklin/Bancroft Passy footprint → Trocadéro diplomatic landscape → École Militaire/Dreyfus public consequence → Hôtel Beauharnais → Invalides state-security setting → Mata Hari’s former hotel block → Arc de Triomphe/Champs public-image coda.

**Approximate walk:** 9–11 km.

**Why it earns a route:** Spy remains a strong desired theme and can own western Paris’s monumental/diplomatic geography. It earns a full route only if the Passy marker and Mata Hari block remain physically legible; otherwise replacements must be found before narration work.

**Absorbs/kills:** preserves old Route 09’s mechanisms, adds famous landmarks as state/display connective tissue. Does not borrow Lutetia from Occupied Paris or generic embassies.

### 9. Who Tells the Love Story? — Public Intimacy and Parisian Myth

**Thesis:** Paris turns private relationships into addresses, rituals, memorial façades, mythological images, and photographs that simplify what they preserve.

**Anchor:** Pont des Arts → Héloïse–Abélard → Fontaine Médicis.

**Spine:** Mur des Je t’aime if open, otherwise rue Chaptal exterior context → Colette/Palais-Royal → Pont des Arts → Héloïse–Abélard façade → Fontaine Médicis → Rodin exterior/garden perimeter context → Bir-Hakeim and Eiffel image-making.

**Approximate walk:** 10–11 km without museum interiors.

**Why it earns a route:** Bir-Hakeim now functions as the visible contemporary payoff to a day about converting relationships into Parisian images, not as a generic Eiffel photograph.

**Absorbs/kills:** rebuilds old Route 01 exterior-first. Kills museum dependence and Pont-Neuf repetition.

### 10. The Future Arrives at the Door — Art Nouveau and Urban Thresholds

**Thesis:** Around 1900, public transit, apartment living, retail, and private display used new materials to make modernity recognizable at the threshold.

**Anchor:** Porte Dauphine Guimard entrance and Lavirotte façade.

**Spine:** Porte Dauphine → Castel Béranger → rue Agar/rue La Fontaine → Hôtel Guimard/Mezzara exteriors if visible → Seine crossing → Lavirotte → Félix Potin rue de Rennes commercial coda.

**Approximate walk:** 9–11 km.

**Why it earns a route:** Félix Potin adds the missing commercial chapter and enough eastward movement to turn a compact façade cluster into a full thematic day, subject to confirming the exact visible argument.

**Absorbs/kills:** expands old Route 08. Kills repetitive Guimard entrances and any claim of unverified interior access.

### 11. Who Watches, Who Works? — Paris on Stage After Dark

**Thesis:** Parisian entertainment turns architecture, performers, workers, audiences, gender, and urban reputation into a nightly production.

**Anchor:** Palais Garnier to Pigalle/Montmartre performance corridor.

**Spine:** Palais Garnier exterior → Folies Bergère → Casino de Paris → Moulin Rouge → Madame Arthur → Lapin Agile exterior → Sacré-Cœur tourist-stage coda.

**Approximate walk:** 7–9 km including Montmartre ascent.

**Why it earns a route:** This is broader and stronger than a three-façade burlesque module. Garnier establishes spectacle as an urban institution; Pigalle and Montmartre shift toward cabaret labor, drag, nightlife, and the city performing itself for visitors.

**Absorbs/kills:** absorbs old Route 10 and the most useful performance/night material from Cinema and old Route 04. Kills Crazy Horse as a remote expensive appendage. Detailed research must keep Lapin Agile/chanson distinct rather than falsely calling every venue burlesque.

### 12. Who Translates the City? — Cosmopolitan North and East

**Thesis:** Migration and cultural exchange change streets through businesses, language, art, performance, and institutions that translate stories without making communities into tourist exhibits.

**Anchor:** Belleville and the Goutte-d’Or/ICI cluster.

**Spine:** Belleville crossroads/rue Dénoyez → Parc de Belleville overlook as city-of-arrivals connective → Bouffes du Nord → Passage Brady → Goutte-d’Or commercial streets → ICI Léon exterior/programme context.

**Approximate walk:** 8–10 km.

**Why it earns a route:** The northern/eastern line has enough social and physical variation, and Parc de Belleville gives breath without replacing people with scenery.

**Absorbs/kills:** preserves old Route 11, exterior-first. Grande Arche is killed as a compulsory stop; Stalingrad is not used to observe residents as evidence.

### 13. Before Paris Eats — Markets, Supply, and Vanished Food Systems

**Thesis:** Food reaches the Parisian table through timed labor, price systems, wholesale memory, and markets that shift between provisioning and leisure.

**Anchor:** Aligre/Beauvau and Les Halles.

**Spine:** Aligre/Beauvau → Marché Bastille when operating or Canal Arsenal produce/transport connective → Enfants Rouges → former central-market streets → Bourse de Commerce/Les Halles finale.

**Approximate walk:** 8–10 km.

**Why it earns a route:** A real morning operating window, multiple distinct market forms, and the vanished wholesale center produce a complete story rather than a tasting crawl.

**Absorbs/kills:** preserves old Route 12. Kills Saint-Quentin as an extra market checklist item and all restaurant obligations.

### 14. The Metabolism of a City — Water, Pressure, Flood, and Waste

**Thesis:** Paris survives through sources, storage, distribution, public access, flood measurement, and waste systems usually hidden behind ordinary surfaces.

**Anchor:** western water works and the Zouave flood gauge.

**Spine:** Square Lamartine artesian fountain → Passy reservoir exterior → Auteuil pumping works exterior → Wallace fountain → Seine river infrastructure → Pont de l’Alma/Zouave → sewer-system surface markers/terminus without museum entry.

**Approximate walk:** 8–10 km.

**Why it earns a route:** The western concentration supports a continuous system story even with museum interiors excluded. The Seine becomes both source/risk and visible payoff.

**Absorbs/kills:** rebuilds old Route 13. Éléonore belongs to modern horror; canal navigation belongs to Route 15 below. Kills the obsolete Pavillon de l’Eau museum assumption.

### 15. The Canal Changes Jobs — Navigation, Industry, Housing, Leisure

**Thesis:** A working transport corridor and its banks keep changing ownership and use without ceasing to be infrastructure.

**Anchor:** Récollets locks to La Villette.

**Spine:** Bastille/Port de l’Arsenal optional opening → covered canal trace → Temple mouth → Récollets locks → Bassin de la Villette/Rotonde → MK2 and northern warehouses → Grande Halle/La Villette.

**Approximate walk:** 10–11 km with Arsenal; 7–8 km without it.

**Why it earns a route:** This is the strongest geography-led walk in the portfolio: the water line itself produces the sequence from navigation to reuse and public culture.

**Absorbs/kills:** preserves and lengthens old Route 18 only through a thematically valid southern opening. No cruise, bathing, or guaranteed lock movement.

### 16. The Northwestern Belt — Rail Cuts, New Parks, and Respectable Nature

**Thesis:** The 17th arrondissement’s parks and promenades are not leftover nature: they occupy former rail, logistics, fortification, and redevelopment landscapes designed to make urban change feel settled and green.

**Anchor:** Parc Clichy-Batignolles–Martin-Luther-King and the Petite Ceinture/Pereire rail landscape.

**Spine:** Batignolles historic village streets → Square des Batignolles → Parc Clichy-Batignolles → accessible Petite Ceinture 17th section → Promenade Pereire → Porte Maillot/fortification edge or Parc Monceau finale, whichever produces the stronger verified line.

**Approximate walk:** 8–10 km.

**Why it earns a route:** This repairs genuine 17th-arrondissement undercoverage through a legible landscape story, not ward-box ticking. Old and new parks, active/redundant rail geometry, and the outer-city edge provide materially different chapters.

**Absorbs/kills:** new landscape commission. The old Paris Reads full route is killed; its best library, shop, and bouquiniste material survives as a compact wet-weather module outside the twenty walking days.

### 17. Paris Learns to See Itself — Cinema, Projection, and the Street

**Thesis:** Cinema does not merely use Paris as scenery; projection technologies, repertory cinemas, and famous screen locations teach viewers how to recognize the city.

**Anchor:** Pathé exterior/institutional history and Champo repertory cluster.

**Spine:** Fondation Pathé exterior → Mouffetard/Latin Quarter movement → Saint-Étienne-du-Mont screen-memory stop → Champo/Champollion → Seine → Louvre screen identity → Grand Rex façade or another active projection-house finale.

**Approximate walk:** 8–10 km.

**Why it earns a route:** It connects apparatus/institution, active exhibition culture, and the rewriting of public places by film. A current screening can be an optional evening payoff, not a prerequisite for the walk.

**Absorbs/kills:** rebuilds old Route 16. Belphégor remains with Route 4; Catacombs horror remains with Route 3. Kills weak location trivia and La Pagode unless reopening is verified.

### 18. The Elevated East — Coulée Verte, Viaduct, and Imperial Edge

**Thesis:** Eastern Paris repeatedly repurposed linear infrastructure and monumental sites, turning rail, workshops, exposition architecture, and military edges into new public landscapes without erasing their origins.

**Anchor:** Coulée verte René-Dumont and Porte Dorée façade.

**Spine:** Bastille/Viaduc des Arts → Coulée verte elevated section → Jardin de Reuilly → rail trench/eastern continuation → Porte Dorée colonial façade/Marchand monument → lac Daumesnil/Bois de Vincennes edge.

**Approximate walk:** 9–11 km.

**Why it earns a route:** It provides a real 12th-arrondissement walking day, a major green corridor, and a defensible final confrontation between reused infrastructure, exposition-era imperial display, and the city’s landscaped edge.

**Absorbs/kills:** absorbs the eastern block of old Route 20 into a landscape-led walk. The colonial façade keeps its own substantial chapter; the route must not imply the Coulée verte “redeems” empire.

### 19. Southern Margins — Bièvre, Petite Ceinture, and Parks of the 13th/14th

**Thesis:** Southern Paris reveals watercourses, railways, quarries, worker districts, and planted spaces that the monumental center tends to hide.

**Anchor:** Butte-aux-Cailles/Bièvre traces and Petite Ceinture–Montsouris landscapes.

**Spine:** Seine/BnF edge → new 13th urban fabric → Butte-aux-Cailles and Bièvre markers → Cité Florale → Petite Ceinture accessible section → Parc Montsouris → Cité Universitaire perimeter.

**Approximate walk:** 9–11 km.

**Why it earns a route:** This is justified geographic coverage: the landscapes themselves tell how the city buried water, abandoned rail, and built new forms around older ground. It also repairs major 13th-arrondissement undercoverage.

**Absorbs/kills:** takes non-horror Montsouris/rail material that Route 3 should not overload. No duplicate quarry narration beyond a brief geological handoff.

### 20. Western Green Machinery — Petite Ceinture, Parks, and the Seine

**Thesis:** Western Paris’s polished residential image is crossed by retired rail, former industrial land, modern parks, and river infrastructure that expose how carefully “green” urban nature is constructed.

**Anchor:** Petite Ceinture 15th/16th and Parc André-Citroën.

**Spine:** Parc Georges-Brassens → Petite Ceinture 15th accessible section → Parc André-Citroën → Seine riverfront → Île aux Cygnes → Bir-Hakeim structure → Petite Ceinture 16th or Porte d’Auteuil landscape finish, depending lawful continuity.

**Approximate walk:** 9–11 km.

**Why it earns a route:** It gives the 15th a real day, joins two important parks to rail and river infrastructure, and uses Eiffel/Bir-Hakeim views as evidence of engineered landscape rather than a greatest-hits finale.

**Absorbs/kills:** absorbs unused Petite Ceinture/nature ambitions and the generic Bir-Hakeim photo stop. It does not duplicate Route 9’s romance narration or Route 14’s water-system chapter.

## Deliberately killed as independent routes

- **Paris of Illusions:** excellent individual waypoints, insufficient full-route identity after 145 rue La Fayette transfers to modern horror. Quincampoix, Chapon, and Buren become optional modules or replacements, not an obligation.
- **Paris Reads as a full exterior walking day:** its best experiences are interiors, browsing, and weather-sensitive stalls rather than a persuasive 7–11 km street arc. Preserve BnF Ovale, Jousseaume, Mazarine, and the bouquinistes as a compact wet-weather/book block, not one of the twenty walking days.
- **Scientific Paris as an exterior walk:** the strongest old material depends on museum instruments, demonstrations, and galleries. Preserve it for free-museum day; do not manufacture a scientist-address route.
- **Colonial Afterlives as a cross-city walk:** the old northwestern and eastern blocks do not share walkable geography. Solitude/Fers becomes a protected standalone module or future ingredient; Porte Dorée joins the eastern landscape route. No false continuous route.
- **Paris Below as a paid-attraction route:** museum/interior planning is separated. Its surface geology and horror material now support Route 3.
- **Modern After Dark as a two-stop module:** rebuilt into Route 4 with enough physically legible anchors.
- **Standalone compact Burlesque:** widened into the stronger “Paris on Stage” route while preserving labor/gaze distinctions.
- **Standalone museum-led Scientific and Cinema itineraries:** science moves to free-museum day; cinema survives only because street exhibition culture and active façades can support a walk.
