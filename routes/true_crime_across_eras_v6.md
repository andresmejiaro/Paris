# True crime across eras — candidate atlas and route branch

Controller correction, 3 October 2026. The portfolio should not treat “modern
true crime” as one museum visit plus Diana, nor imply that private or altered
crime sites are automatically worthless. Absence, demolition, disputed evidence
and media distortion can be the subject—provided the address is exact and the
route states what the pavement can and cannot prove.

This document is a **candidate atlas**, not a finished narration. It expands the
bench across early-modern, nineteenth-, twentieth- and late-twentieth-century
cases while protecting the distinction between documented location, surviving
fabric, institutional collection, legal finding and public myth.
Narration uses `mystery_narration_protocol_v6.md`; graphic or morally difficult
facts are not excluded when they explain evidence, contradiction or the birth
of a legend.

### Why this branch may work

The argument is not “five notorious places.” It is the construction of modern
crime knowledge:

1. **BiLiPo:** crime is edited, serialized, classified and remembered.
2. **Police Museum:** bodies and suspects become photographs, measurements,
   traces and exhibits.
3. **Former 36:** files become institutional reputation and screen mythology.
4. **Landru arrest ground:** aliases, commercial records, recognition,
   anthropometric files and the small notebook turn disappearance into a case.
5. **Rue Ordener:** automobiles, firearms, fingerprints, press spectacle and
   contested attribution create the modern mobile “public enemy.”

The ending matters because the Bonnot story contains mystery rather than merely
certainty: later claims disputed who fired at the collector, the gang’s own
letter weaponized fingerprints and Bertillon’s system, and cultural retellings
converted violent acts into a rebellious legend.

## Strong case satellite — Violette Nozière

### 9 rue de Madagascar, 12th arrondissement

**Decision: KEEP for waypoint investigation.** This is the strongest additional
case because it combines an exact address, a documented domestic crime, survival
of one intended victim, conflicting public narratives, sensational press,
alleged incest, a death sentence, commutation, release and eventual legal
rehabilitation.

The Musée Carnavalet’s current research identifies the address and situates the
family in a working railway district. Criminocorpus preserves interrogation,
press, trial and cultural materials showing how quickly the case exceeded the
apartment: Violette was variously made into monster, liar, victim, sexual rebel,
surrealist symbol and rehabilitated offender.

The mystery is not “did nothing happen?” Established facts include poisoning,
her father’s death, her mother’s survival, conviction and later rehabilitation.
The route’s unresolved questions concern motive, the incest allegation, how the
surviving mother and courts framed it, and why media/cultural actors needed
Violette to embody incompatible stories.

Stand only on public pavement. A current residential façade is context, not
permission to identify an apartment, photograph occupants or claim surviving
interior fabric. This can be a 20–30 minute satellite near the 12th-arrondissement
day, but it does not become an Elevated East chapter; separate the narratives.

Sources: [Musée Carnavalet 2025–26 research dossier](https://carnavalet.paris.fr/sites/default/files/2025-09/DP-Musee%20Carnavalet-Les%20gens%20de%20Paris-web.pdf),
[Criminocorpus case documents](https://criminocorpus.org/fr/expositions/presumees-coupables-du-14e-au-20e-siecle/lempoisonneuse/),
[Criminocorpus on press and political myth](https://criminocorpus.org/fr/expositions/detective-fabrique-de-crimes/ordre-public-ordre-moral/),
[Archives de Paris collection context](https://www.paris.fr/pages/historique-missions-fonds-et-collections-d-archives-2353/).

## Other twentieth-century candidates

### Landru — 76 rue de Rochechouart

**Conditional core for the northbound branch.** The Paris address matters as
arrest/evidence ground, not as the place where the Gambais killings occurred.
Criminocorpus documents recognition in a shop, the delivery trail to the alias
“Guillet,” the arrest, search, identity and notebook that structured the case.
This is stronger than pretending a Paris apartment contains the absent Gambais
crime scene.

Source: [Criminocorpus, Landru case](https://criminocorpus.org/fr/outils/affaires-criminelles/affaire-landru-1921/).

### Bande à Bonnot — rue Ordener

**Conditional finale.** The December 1911 armed attack is an early motorized
crime/media/police event. Criminocorpus preserves the gang letter, fingerprint
taunt and later dispute over attribution. Exact numbered ground needs archival
pin verification; surviving-bank claims are prohibited until checked.

Sources: [Criminocorpus, Garnier letter](https://criminocorpus.org/fr/bibliotheque/doc/162/),
[Criminocorpus, crime complaint and Bertillon taunt](https://complaintes.criminocorpus.org/fr/complainte/as-tu-gaffe-bertillon-ou-laffaire-de-la-rue-ordene/),
[Criminocorpus, history-to-myth analysis](https://criminocorpus.org/fr/ref/25/18018/).

### Marcel Petiot — 21 rue Le Sueur

**Case essential; street waypoint weak.** The crime and the address are exact,
and the Occupation context, fraudulent escape network, discovered remains,
victim identification and Petiot’s “Resistance” claim demand serious treatment.
But the original hôtel particulier was demolished and replaced. The strongest
encounter is therefore Police Museum/BiLiPo evidence, with rue Le Sueur only an
optional absence stop—not a reconstructed chamber-of-horrors narration.

Source: [Criminocorpus Petiot press archive](https://criminocorpus.org/fr/bibliotheque/doc/338/).

### Mesrine, Thierry Paulin and Guy Georges

**Institutional chapters, not yet street waypoints.** They belong in the modern
police/media/forensics story because investigation methods, press identities and
institutional memory are meaningful. No specific public site has yet cleared
exact-location, surviving-payoff and non-duplication gates. Their absence from
the street spine is a research status, not a ban.

The City itself identifies Mesrine and the pursuit of Guy Georges among cases
associated with the former 36. Source: [Ville de Paris on the Police judiciaire](https://www.paris.fr/pages/nouvelles-enquetes-aux-batignolles-pour-le-36-5133).

## Earlier true crime — use cases, trials and objects rather than an “old crime” dump

Earlier centuries are not absent; they require a different physical strategy.
Many execution grounds, prisons and crime houses vanished or already belong to
Revolutionary/Medieval routes. The Police Museum’s broad collection and the
judicial/archive layer can support:

- **Ravaillac and regicide:** crime, torture, punishment and state spectacle;
- **the Affair of the Poisons:** testimony, poison, court secrecy and political
  protection;
- **the Diamond Necklace affair:** fraud, credibility, monarchy and print;
- **Vidocq:** offender, informer, police institution and self-authored legend;
- **Lacenaire:** murder, trial performance, memoir and criminal celebrity;
- **Troppmann:** mass murder and the rise of mass-circulation *fait divers*;
- **the Gouffé trunk case:** identification, decomposition, transport and early
  forensic narrative.

These are not yet outdoor waypoints. A finite Police Museum object audit should
determine which can become actual chapters. Conciergerie/Palais de Justice may
provide legal geography, but generic prison stone cannot authenticate every
case tried or detained there.

## Late twentieth century and contemporary bench

- **Diana / Alma:** KEEP, already assigned. The strongest fact–finding–myth
  chapter and a real public memorial transformation.
- **Baron Empain kidnapping:** institutionally important, but the portfolio does
  not yet have a public object/site that earns a waypoint.
- **Stavisky:** powerful crime-politics-media material; needs a Paris object that
  does more than stand for a scandal whose death occurred in Chamonix.
- **organized robbery and the gang des postiches:** visually/media-rich but needs
  a specific bank/event site with verified surviving payoff rather than a generic
  bank façade.
- **terrorist attacks:** not folded casually into “true crime.” Bataclan,
  Hypercacher, Charlie Hebdo and other memorial locations require their own
  remembrance/security framework and are not atmosphere for this module.
- **unsolved or disputed deaths:** eligible when an exact public site and a
  responsible evidence ledger exist. Unsolved status is a reason for better
  epistemic design, not automatic exclusion or automatic inclusion.

## Selection rule revised

The previous wording leaned too close to “private address equals unethical and
therefore unusable.” The actual gate is stricter and more useful:

A true-crime waypoint may pass when it has:

1. an exact, sourced location;
2. lawful public observation;
3. a physical presence **or meaningful documented absence**;
4. a case question that changes the route’s argument;
5. an evidence ledger separating fact, report, dispute and myth;
6. no instruction to invade, identify or photograph present residents;
7. a reason to be there that cannot be delivered equally well from the Police
   Museum or BiLiPo.

Graphic facts, offender behavior, institutional failure and victim experience
are not forbidden. They must be relevant, sourced and narrated without pretending
that horror itself proves a disputed theory.
